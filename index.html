Section 1 — Background

Cost Structure & Transport Capacity

Where inventory is stored and when it moves affect operating costs.

External warehouse

Space occupied

Storage charges depend on the space occupied by inventory at the external warehouse (EX).

Cargo terminal

Around 2 weeks free

Arrivals at the cargo terminal (CT) have a free storage period. Storage charges begin once it expires.

The exact free storage period varies by supplier.

Transportation

Limited capacity

Capacity limits apply to CT collection and transfers between warehouses in either direction.

Planning implication: Coordinate collection timing, storage location and transfers within the available transport capacity.

Section 1 — Demand Characteristics

Project Orders & Walk-in Demand

Demand differs in both advance visibility and service requirements.

Advance project orders

Visibility
Large project orders are known several days in advance, allowing inventory to be positioned before delivery.
Service
Prioritise delivery by the committed date.
If unfulfilled
Unmet orders carry over as backlog demand. Delays harm customer experience and may incur substantial compensation costs.

Backlog demand

Unmet project orders are carried forward as backlog until fulfilled.

Walk-in customer demand

Visibility
Demand is not known in advance. The quantity becomes known when customers arrive.
Service
Customers require same-day fulfilment from local stock.
If unfulfilled
When local inventory is insufficient, the sale is lost instead of carried over.

Lost-sales treatment

Serve demand from available local inventory. Record any unmet quantity as lost sales for that day.

General demand setting. The appliance (APPL) backtest shown later excludes walk-in demand.

Section 1 — Business Challenge

Why Is This Complex? The Decisions Are Coupled

Many SKUs share the same trucks and warehouse space. A decision for one order changes the capacity available for others, across locations and days.

Manager's decisionImmediate benefitConsequence elsewhere
Move stock to North Point todayPrepare for a project order due later in the week.Use truck capacity now and occupy space that other urgent orders may need.
Move slower-moving stock elsewhereRelease space for incoming or urgently needed goods.Create another transfer and consume capacity at the receiving warehouse.
Use external storage for overflowRelieve pressure on internal warehouse space.Add handling costs and storage charges that accumulate while goods remain there.

Managers must balance future order readiness with today's capacity needs.

When internal capacity is tight, external storage may become necessary. Repeated reliance on it can create substantial ongoing costs.

Section 1 — Business Challenge

The Cargo Terminal Timing Trade-off

The free storage period provides a useful buffer, but collection timing also affects warehouse congestion and delivery readiness.

Collect early

Avoid future terminal storage charges and allow time to prepare goods for delivery.

Warehouse pressure

Use space that urgent demand may need, potentially pushing other inventory into external storage.

Use CT as a buffer

Keep goods within their free storage period without occupying internal or external warehouse space.

Planning opportunity

Delay warehouse entry until space and demand justify collection, while leaving time for processing.

Wait too long

Collection deadlines may cluster, exceeding the available transport or receiving capacity.

Service and cost risk

Containers may overstay, or goods may reach the warehouse too late for inspection, packaging and delivery.

High-turnover SKUs need timely collection to support customer demand.

Managers must jointly plan collection, transfers and storage across SKUs and days, allowing for internal inspection and packaging before delivery.

Section 3 — Mathematical Formulation

Full Theoretical Model: Formulation

Sets & Indices

  • $\mathcal{I}$ — set of SKUs
  • $\mathcal{W} = \{\mathrm{NP,TM}\}$ — internal warehouses
  • $\mathcal{C}$ — containers at the Cargo Terminal
  • $\mathcal{T} = \{1,\ldots,H\}$ — planning horizon ($H=7$ days)
  • $\mathcal{H} \subseteq \mathcal{T}$ — holidays (warehouse closed)
  • $\mathcal{W}^{+} = \mathcal{T}\setminus\mathcal{H}$ — working days
  • $\mathcal{P}$ — active project/prepick order lines

Key Decision Variables

  • $\delta_{c,w,t} \in \{0,1\}$ — collect container $c$ to warehouse $w$ on day $t$
  • $y^{w\to w'}_{i,t} \geq 0$ — transfer SKU $i$ from $w$ to $w'$
  • $x^{\mathrm{EX}\to w}_{i,t},\; x^{w\to\mathrm{EX}}_{i,t} \geq 0$ — EX retrieve / offload
  • $s_{i,w,t} \geq 0$ — walk-in lost sales; $\;B_{i,w,t} \geq 0$ — unfulfilled order carryover
  • $I_{i,w,t} \in \mathbb{R}$ — signed inventory position; $\;\eta_{c,t} \in [0,1]$ — CT overstay indicator

Objective Function

$$ \min \sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}} \gamma^{t-1}\!\Bigl[ \underbrace{h^{\mathrm{EX}}_d \!\sum_i v_i I^{\mathrm{EX}}_{i,t}}_{\text{(A) EX holding}} + \underbrace{h^{\mathrm{EX}}_a \!\sum_i v_i x^{w\to\mathrm{EX}}_{i,t}}_{\text{(B) EX arrival}} + \underbrace{\sum_w R^{\mathrm{EX}\to w}_t}_{\text{(C) EX retrieval}} + \underbrace{\sum_w R^{w\to\mathrm{EX}}_t}_{\text{(D) EX offload}} $$ $$ + \underbrace{\sum_{i,w} p_i s_{i,w,t}}_{\text{(E) Lost sales}} + \underbrace{\sum_{i,w} b_i B_{i,w,t}}_{\text{(F) Unfulfilled orders}} + \underbrace{\sum_{i,w} \omega^w v_i \delta^+_{i,w,t}}_{\text{(G) Space opp.}} + \underbrace{\sum_{c,w} F^{\mathrm{CT}}_{|V_c|} \delta_{c,w,t}}_{\text{(I) CT transport}} + \underbrace{\sum_c h^{\mathrm{CT}} V_c \eta_{c,t}}_{\text{(J) CT overstay}} \Bigr]$$ $$+ \gamma^{H-1}\underbrace{\sum_{i,w}\bigl[\gamma^- \delta^{-,H}_{i,w} + \gamma^+ \omega^w v_i \delta^{+,H}_{i,w}\bigr]}_{\text{(H) Terminal value}}$$

Discount Factor $\gamma^{t-1}$

  • Weights day-1 costs at full value; progressively discounts future days
  • Reflects decreasing forecast reliability as the horizon extends
  • Future decisions will be revised as the horizon rolls forward each day

Section 3 — Mathematical Formulation

Full Theoretical Model: Key Constraints

Inventory Balance

Tracks goods in = goods out at each warehouse every day

$$I_{i,w,t} = I_{i,w,t-1} - B_{i,w,t-1} + r_{i,w,t} + \mathrm{CT}_{i,w,t} + y^{w'\!\to w}_{i,t} - y^{w\to w'}_{i,t} + x^{\mathrm{EX}\!\to w}_{i,t} - x^{w\to\mathrm{EX}}_{i,t} - D^{\mathrm{det}}_{i,w,t} - D^{\mathrm{sto}}_{i,w,t} + u_{i,w,t} + s_{i,w,t}$$

Warehouse Capacity

Total volume in each warehouse cannot exceed its floor space

$$\sum_i v_i \max(0, I_{i,w,t}) \leq K^w \quad \forall w,\; t$$

Internal Transfer Capacity

Daily truck trips limit how much can move between NP and TM

$$\sum_i \frac{v_i}{V_P} y^{w\to w'}_{i,t} \leq n^{\mathrm{trips}} \cdot P^{\mathrm{trip}} \quad \forall t \in \mathcal{W}^+$$

CT Collection Rules

Each container collected at most once; not before it arrives at CT

$$\sum_w \sum_{t} \delta_{c,w,t} \leq 1 \quad \forall c; \qquad \delta_{c,w,t} = 0 \text{ if } t < \tau_c$$

CT Intake Capacity

Limits how many containers each warehouse can receive per day

$$\sum_{i,c} v_i\, a_{i,c}\, \delta_{c,w,t} \leq P^{\mathrm{intake}}_w \quad \forall w,\; t \in \mathcal{W}^+$$

CT Overstay Indicator

Flags containers left at CT past their free storage period — triggers penalty

$$\eta_{c,t} \leq 1 - \sum_w \sum_{\tau \leq t} \delta_{c,w,\tau}, \quad t \geq \bar{\tau}^{\mathrm{FSP}}_c$$

Section 2 — Proposed Solution

A Rolling-Horizon MILP Model

We formulate the problem as a mixed-integer linear optimisation model, updated each working day.

Plan 7 working days. Implement only the first.

Working dayDay 1Day 2Day 3Day 4Day 5Day 6Day 7Day 8
TodayExecutePlanPlanPlanPlanPlanPlan—
Next dayDoneExecuteReplanReplanReplanReplanReplanPlan

The window moves forward by one working day. Future decisions are revised using updated inputs.

The daily cycle

  1. Optimise the next seven working days.
  2. Implement only the first day's decisions.
  3. Update the inputs and solve again on the next working day.

Container collection

Which containers to collect, when to collect them and where to send them.

North Point / Tuen Mun transfers

How much of each SKU to move between the two internal warehouses.

External warehouse movements

Which goods to retrieve from or offload to the external warehouse.

How Do We Know the Model Is Good? Baselines

We compare our MILP against three baselines — all sharing the same data, cost parameters, opening inventory, and APPL-only scope.

Why Greedy?

  • Mimics how a busy manager reacts to today's needs
  • Collects containers when urgency arises (demand or FSP)
  • Represents a reasonable "human heuristic" baseline

Why Rule-Based?

  • Simple policy: always collect containers on arrival day
  • Common in practice — feels safe and predictable
  • Ignores warehouse space readiness, may inflate EX costs

Why Actual Operations?

  • Reconstructed from Towngas's own SAP data
  • Shows the real cost of current practice
  • Most operationally meaningful comparison
